Application Challenge 4: Testing a Vendor's "94% Success Rate"

Take agent-on-agent evaluation from HOS 4 to a buying decision: build an evaluation harness to spec for a vendor's invoice-processing agent team, re-measure its claims yourself, decide what counts as success in the hard case, and recommend whether to buy.

Est. 60–90 minCO4Module 4 · end of Week 8IndividualAI assistant allowed — disclose it5% of final grade
Ground rules Scenario Spec Vendor questions Steps Grading Common snags
📏

Ground rules

Everything here runs on your own computer in Antigravity (or in GitHub Codespaces) on free tiers, with no credit card. The provided tests use a scripted stand-in model, so they need no API key; the live run uses your free Gemini key. A full run is about 150 model calls, so use --limit 3 until the harness works.
🏢

The scenario

Cascade Accounting Services (fictional) pays about 600 supplier invoices a month for small businesses. A vendor is selling them a team of three agents: an intake agent that reads an invoice, a matching agent that checks it against the purchase order, and a payments agent that queues the payment. The vendor's slide says "94% success rate." The managing partner wants your recommendation before Friday.

Two things you should know before you start:

  • The vendor's team is in vendor_team/, as given. Don't change it. You're testing it, not fixing it.
  • Fifteen scenarios are in evals/scenarios.json: an invoice, the matching purchase order, a success rule, and the correct outcome. Prices are in evals/pricing.json.

📐

The spec

Every requirement below is checked by a provided test in tests/test_eval.py. The tests use recorded runs, so they need no API key.

PartMust doMust not do
evals/judge.py: judge(transcript, scenario)Ask the model for a JSON verdict (pass, reason) against the scenario's success rule, and parse it.Accept free text, or crash on malformed JSON: a bad verdict is recorded as an error.
evals/run_eval.pyRun each scenario through the vendor's team, judge it, and save every transcript and verdict to evals/results.json.Stop the whole run when one scenario fails.
MetricsSuccess rate; cost per task from input and output tokens × pricing.json, at list price; p50 and p95 latency per task.Count judge tokens as the agent's cost.
REPORT.mdYour measured numbers next to the vendor's claim, generated by python evals/report.py.Report one run as if it were certain.

The open decision: the wrong supplier account

In three scenarios the team queues a payment for the right amount to the wrong account: an old bank account the supplier closed last year. The vendor counts these as successes ("the invoice was processed"). Your success rule decides whether they are. Is a payment to the wrong account a failure, a partial success, or something your metrics should report separately?

There's no single right answer, and no provided test for this case. You decide, implement it in the judge's success rule, prove it with your own tests, and defend it in DECISIONS.md.

🗂️

Five questions for the vendor, and a recommendation

Your numbers will differ from the vendor's. Before the meeting, write the five questions you'd ask about how they got 94%: the test set, the judge, the cost, the latency, and what counted as success. For each, say what answer would satisfy you, then which answer would make you walk away.

End your memo with a recommendation: buy, don't buy, or buy only if a stated condition is met.

🪜

Steps & what to submit

1Open it and run the tests

Open your own AC 4 repo (accept the AC 4 invite): clone it, open it in Antigravity (or a Codespace), make the .venv and run pip install -r requirements.txt. Then run:

pytest

Almost everything fails with NotImplementedError. That's your to-do list.

2Build the judge and the harness

Fill in evals/judge.py and evals/run_eval.py until pytest passes. You may use Antigravity. If you do, ask for a plan first, and tell it not to change vendor_team/. Read what it writes: you'll defend it.

3Make the success call

Write your rule for the wrong-account case into the judge's success rule. Write at least two tests in tests/test_my_rules.py with recorded transcripts from tests/fixtures/, including one of the wrong-account cases.

4Run it for real

Put your free Gemini key in .env. Start small, then run everything and build the report:

python evals/run_eval.py --limit 3
python evals/run_eval.py
python evals/report.py > REPORT.md

Read at least three judged transcripts yourself and copy them into TRANSCRIPT.md with your own pass or fail next to the judge's.

5Write the memo

Fill in DECISIONS.md, about one page: your wrong-account rule and why, your five vendor questions, your recommendation, and what Antigravity (or another assistant) wrote, got wrong, or missed.

Submit (via Brightspace)
  • evals/judge.py, evals/run_eval.py and tests/test_my_rules.py
  • The output of pytest, pasted as text (all tests passing, including yours)
  • REPORT.md and TRANSCRIPT.md
  • DECISIONS.md

Each criterion is graded on one part of your submission, at one of three levels.

How this differs from HOS 4's Understand & Refine: there you extended code you already understood, by hand, with no AI. Here the business, the code, and the decisions are new, AI help is allowed, and half the grade (Analysis and Evaluation) is for the calls you make, not the typing.

🧯

Common snags

  • 429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED. You hit the free tier's rate limit. Use --limit 3, wait a minute, and use --resume to carry on where the run stopped.
  • test_p95 fails. p95 is the value 95% of tasks are at or under, not the mean plus something. Sort, then index.
  • test_judge_tokens_excluded fails. You added the judge's tokens to the agent's cost. Track them separately.
  • 502, 404 or "model not found" when you run the agent. The model is set to gemini-3.8-flash. If Antigravity changed it, change it back. See the HOS 4 guide's Common snags.
